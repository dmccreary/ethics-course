Here is the outline of the story:

Studies have shown that many animals have an inherent concept of fairness.
Monkeys get upset if they get less rewards than their peers.
What is the relationship between the concept of fairness and ethics?
What is most interesting is that what we perceive as fair changes over time.
Let's look at three examples.

The first example is gender fairness.  Imagine two children, a boy and a girl.
They are both very similar in their height, hair color, skin color and clothing.  But
in the past, the girl would be denied the right to education, the ability to own property, the ability to vote and the other legal rights.

Over time, many societies around the world have decide that this gender discrimination was unfair.
Laws were put into place to give both boys and girls equal opportunities.

The second example is racial fairness.  Imagine two children, both girls. 
They are both very similar in their height, and clothing.   But one girl
has slightly darker skin.  Not a big difference.  Just a slightly
darker color.  In the past, the girl with lighter skin often
had more opportunities than the girl with darker skin.  And the
darker the skin, the more disadvantages the girl had.  The girl
with the darker skin has fewer rights, fewer opportunities to become educated
and was discriminated against because of the color of her skin alone.

Over time, many societies around the world have decide that this discrimination was unfair.
Laws were put into place to give these girls equal opportunities, regardless
of the color of their skin.

Although both these examples seem obvious of fairness today, it is critical to remember that this was not always the case.  In many countries of the world, both gender and race discrimination are still present.

The third example is much more relevant today.  It is about invisible lines that we cannot see.
Imagine two boys.  They are both very similar in their height, color of their skin and their clothing.
But one was originally born on one side of an invisible line, and the other boy
was born on the other side of this line.   Today, these boys might be treated very differently.  One has many opportunities for living in a safe country with ample opportunities for education and where his rights are protected by a society.  He also has the right to move around and visit many parts of the world.  

The other boy unfortunately was born on the other side of the line.  This spot was in a
country that did not have the same opportunities.  Their political system had collapsed
and the country is driven by violent gangs that threten anyone that opposes them.  The boy might be drafted into a children's army and be forced to kill other children around him.

Now we ask the key question.  What is fair?  Are the two boys in the last example being treated fairly?  What is our obligation to treat them fairly?

What we find is that the concept of fairness is not static.  It changes over time and is
based on the conditions around us.  Will the idea of fairness for the third example change
over time and be different for different part of the world?  Yes it will.

However, it is the hope and dreams of all higher-order primates that fairness is
important and with luck, the world will become a more fair place over time.

What helps a country become more fair?  Often it requires to things: 1. an educated population that understands the concepts around ethics and fairness and 2. educated leaders that help everyone promote fairness where they feel it is appropriate.

History has not been kind to people in the past that have tried to promote one group of people over another group.  We can look back and see that many people that have tried to promote hatred of people that were different have been made into villains.  On the other hand, leaders that work hard to promote fairness of all people have often been made into saints and are well regarded by history.

In conclusion, we need to understand that Ethics is situational and although there
are clearly trends, the increase of fairness in our world is not guaranteed.  We
need both education and leaders to make the world a place that is free from hatred and fear.
