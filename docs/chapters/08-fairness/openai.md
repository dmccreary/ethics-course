## People Who Tried to Make the World **More Fair**

1. **Mahatma Gandhi**
   *Actions:* Led India’s independence movement through nonviolent resistance, civil disobedience, and moral persuasion, challenging imperial rule without armed revolt.
   *Historical view:* Widely revered as proof that mass nonviolence can dismantle unjust systems, though historians also note political compromises and limitations of his approach.

2. **Martin Luther King Jr.**
   *Actions:* Organized nonviolent campaigns to end racial segregation and secure voting and civil rights in the United States.
   *Historical view:* Seen as a moral cornerstone of modern democracy; some contemporaries criticized his tactics as disruptive, but history credits him with reshaping U.S. law and norms.

3. **Nelson Mandela**
   *Actions:* Fought apartheid, endured decades of imprisonment, and later guided South Africa toward a multiracial democracy with an emphasis on reconciliation.
   *Historical view:* Regarded as a model of moral leadership under extreme injustice, balancing justice with social stability.

4. **Eleanor Roosevelt**
   *Actions:* Championed human rights globally and played a central role in drafting the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
   *Historical view:* Credited with helping define human dignity as a global moral standard rather than a national privilege.

5. **Frederick Douglass**
   *Actions:* Escaped slavery and became a leading abolitionist, writer, and advocate for equal rights and citizenship.
   *Historical view:* Seen as one of the most powerful moral voices against slavery and systemic dehumanization.

6. **Malala Yousafzai**
   *Actions:* Advocated for girls’ education under Taliban repression and continued globally after surviving an assassination attempt.
   *Historical view:* Viewed as a modern symbol of educational equity and moral courage, especially for children’s rights.

## People Whose Actions Made the World **More Unfair**

1. **Adolf Hitler**
   *Actions:* Led a totalitarian regime responsible for genocide, mass war, and systematic dehumanization.
   *Historical view:* Universally condemned as a central example of how ideology, power, and fear can produce catastrophic injustice.

2. **Joseph Stalin**
   *Actions:* Enforced collectivization, purges, and labor camps that caused millions of deaths.
   *Historical view:* Acknowledged for industrializing the USSR but overwhelmingly criticized for extreme repression and human suffering.

3. **Leopold II**
   *Actions:* Personally controlled the Congo Free State, extracting wealth through forced labor, violence, and terror.
   *Historical view:* Now recognized as one of the most brutal colonial rulers; once praised, now strongly condemned.

4. **Pol Pot**
   *Actions:* Attempted to impose an agrarian utopia in Cambodia through mass executions and forced relocations.
   *Historical view:* Remembered almost exclusively for genocide and ideological extremism detached from human reality.

5. **Benito Mussolini**
   *Actions:* Established a fascist dictatorship, suppressed dissent, and pursued imperial aggression.
   *Historical view:* Seen as a cautionary example of how nationalism and authoritarianism corrode institutions and freedoms.

6. **Francisco Franco**
   *Actions:* Ruled Spain through authoritarian control after a civil war, repressing political opponents for decades.
   *Historical view:* Sometimes defended by supporters for stability, but broadly criticized for systematic repression and delayed democratic development.
